## 6. Positive controls — the proof can fail

The proof is not a rubber stamp; three earlier runs failed and were fixed by fixing the harness, not by weakening an assertion:

1. `run1` failed its cadence check: the driver counted lines in `health-metrics.jsonl` alone, and rotation moved them — a *measurement* bug. Fixed by counting distinct sample timestamps across generations; `run3`/`run4` then read +5 samples/5 s.
2. `run2` and `run3` failed a check that demanded two causal `Memory:` lines *before* the window, but the session-regime line lands on the next 30 s memory tick, i.e. inside window A. Fixed by asserting the causal lines after the windows; `run4` then sees both (`sessions=0`, `sessions=6`).
3. `run3` failed window A's rate bound because a single causal line extrapolated to 30 lines/hour over a 120 s window; window B, not A, owns the steady-state rate.

Before the live proof, the assertion set itself was falsified in unit form: the RED run above (exit 1) and the pre-existing `event-loop-history.test.ts`, which keeps the lag-window change honest (it still passes, 3/3).

- **Residual risk — lag alert not exercised live.** The live proof exercised the heap latch on real readings; the lag latch is covered by unit tests (band, no flapping) and observed in real samples (p50 1 ms / p99 2 ms / max 3 ms on the disposable server), but no live alert was raised on lag because no real sustained lag was produced. - **Residual risk — registry source is best-effort.** `registryEntries` is `null` when no unique registry instance exists (documented); it read 6/6 on the disposable server.

That is deliberate (see the section in `docs/OBSERVABILITY.md`), and the metrics file is the continuous record.
